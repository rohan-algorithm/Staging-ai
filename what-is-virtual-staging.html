What is virtual staging?

Virtual staging is adding furniture and decor to a photo of an empty room, digitally, so buyers browsing a listing can see how the space could be used. Nothing is delivered to the house. Only the photo changes.

By Rohan Rao, founder of RoomGenixAI · Published October 31, 2026

The short version

A photographer shoots the vacant room as usual. Software or an editor then places a sofa, rug, bed, table or desk into that photo, sized to the room and lit to match. The listing shows the staged photo, usually next to the empty original, with a note that it was virtually staged.

Studio example, not a client listing.

Why agents use it

Empty rooms are hard to read in photos. It is difficult to judge whether a king bed fits, where a sofa would go, or whether a spare room works as an office. Furniture gives a sense of scale and purpose. Most buyers first see a home online, so the photos decide whether they book a showing. Virtual staging improves those photos without the cost and logistics of renting real furniture.

How it works

There are two main ways to do it:

  • Hand-edited virtual staging. A designer adds furniture by hand or with 3D models. Results can be very polished, but it usually costs tens of dollars per photo and takes a day or more.
  • AI virtual staging. Software generates the furniture in the photo, usually in a minute or two, for a few dollars or less per photo. Quality has improved fast, but results vary between tools, and some AI tools redraw the whole room instead of only adding furniture. What you can and can't change

Adding furniture and decor to an empty room is the core use and is widely accepted when it's disclosed. Related edits, such as removing clutter or turning a day shot into twilight, are also common but deserve the same disclosure. What you can't do is misrepresent the property: hide damage, remove permanent features like power lines, change the view, or make a room look bigger than it is.

Disclosure

Many MLS boards require virtually staged photos to be labeled, and some want the unstaged original in the listing too. In California, AB 723 has required disclosure and access to the original for digitally altered listing images since January 1, 2026. Rules differ by board and state, so check yours. When virtual staging is the wrong tool

  • When most buyers will walk in from an open house rather than browse online first. Physical furniture is there in person; virtual furniture isn't.
  • When the room has problems furniture would hide. Fix or disclose them instead.
  • When the photo itself is poor. Staging can't fix a dark, crooked or blurry shot, so start with a good, level, well-exposed photo.

Questions

Is virtual staging the same as a 3D render?

No. Virtual staging edits a real photo of the actual room. A 3D render builds the whole scene digitally, often before a home is finished.

Do buyers mind virtual staging?

Buyers generally accept it when it is clearly labeled and the room itself is shown accurately. Problems come from undisclosed edits or photos that change the property.

How long does AI virtual staging take?

With RoomGenixAI a photo is usually ready in a minute or two. Hand-edited services typically take a day or more.

Can I virtually stage an occupied home?

Yes, by removing clutter or personal items first. That is still an altered image, so disclose it the same way.
