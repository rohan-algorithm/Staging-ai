Why vacant DFW listings get staged online

A lot of homes in North Texas hit the market empty: relocation sales, new construction, investor flips and estates. Empty rooms are hard to read in photos. Buyers can't tell whether a king bed fits or where the dining table goes. Physical staging solves that but means renting furniture, scheduling movers and paying for it month after month. Virtual staging puts furniture into the photo instead, so you can show how a room can be used without moving anything into the house.

Matching the style to the house

DFW has a wide mix of housing, and furniture that suits one home can look off in another. A few honest starting points:

  • New-build suburbs such as Frisco, McKinney, Prosper and Celina often have open plans and neutral finishes. Modern or Scandinavian furniture usually matches them.
  • Older Dallas neighborhoods like the M Streets or Lakewood have cottages and homes with classic trim. Traditional or farmhouse tends to fit the architecture.
  • Mid-century ranches, which you'll find in many inner-ring suburbs, can look right with mid-century modern pieces.
  • Higher-end homes may suit luxury staging.

These are rough guides, not rules. You can preview more than one style on the same photo and pick the one that looks right for the house.

Rooms to stage first

If you only stage a few photos, start with the rooms buyers ask about most: the main living area, the primary bedroom and the dining area. A spare room shown as a home office helps buyers who work from home picture it. The studio stages living rooms, bedrooms, dining rooms and home offices.

Photo tips for bright Texas light

  • Balance the windows. Strong sun can blow out windows and leave the room dark. A well-exposed, edited photo gives the best staged result.
  • Shoot from a corner at chest height so the floor and at least two walls are visible. That gives the furniture somewhere to sit.
  • Keep verticals straight. Tilted walls make added furniture look wrong.
  • Clear what you can. Boxes and tools left by movers or builders show up in the result. For occupied homes, see decluttering photos.

Disclosure in North Texas

Most DFW listings go through NTREIS, the North Texas MLS, and Texas licensees are regulated by the Texas Real Estate Commission, which prohibits misleading advertising.

Does NTREIS allow virtually staged photos?

Virtually staged photos are widely used on DFW listings. Published guides disagree about whether the NTREIS rules contain a rule written specifically for virtual staging. Some read the current rules as having none, and others say labeling is required. What the rules do clearly address is photos that misrepresent the property, and putting non-descriptive content such as company branding on listing photos. Local boards have flagged staged photos carrying a vendor's name or logo. The low-risk habit:

  • Say the photos are virtually staged in the remarks or photo captions.
  • Keep the empty original in the listing or available on request.
  • Don't add a vendor name, logo or watermark to the photo.
  • Don't use staging to hide damage or change the room.

Read the current photo rules on ntreis.net or ask your board and broker before you publish, since rules change. RoomGenix doesn't put a label or logo on the file. Our MLS photo rules page is a reminder, not legal advice.
