What to check before you publish

Updated: October 2026 This page is not: an NAR or MLS approval
The one rule that always applies:
Read your board’s current photo rules before you publish. Virtual staging is often allowed when the photo is disclosed and does not hide damage or change the building. RoomGenixAI does not approve a listing, and this page is not legal advice.

1. How boards tend to treat common edits

There is no single national rule. Each MLS board, and some states, set their own. The groups below are a rough guide to how rules commonly treat each kind of edit, not a standard any board has adopted. When your board's rule says something different, follow the board.

Usually allowed with disclosure

Adding furniture and decor

  • Adding furniture, rugs, wall art, lamps and accessories to a vacant room.
  • Staging an empty patio or deck with outdoor furniture.
  • Most boards that allow this still want the photo labeled, and some want the empty original in the listing too.
Disclose, and check the rule first

Removing items or changing the scene

  • Decluttering: removing tenant belongings, boxes or furniture. This is still an altered image. In California, AB 723 requires disclosure for it.
  • Virtual twilight: turning a daytime exterior into a dusk shot.
  • Showing a room used for something else, such as a bedroom staged as an office.
  • Virtual repainting, new flooring or new fixtures. These show changes to the property itself, so many boards treat them more strictly than furniture.
Misleading, so don't

Hiding or inventing facts about the property

  • Hiding damage: water stains, cracks, mold, broken glass.
  • Removing permanent things outside the home, such as power lines, poles or neighboring buildings.
  • Changing the view, or making a room look larger than it is.

2. What is on the staged photo

Boards set their own photo rules. Some want words on the photo, some want words in the public remarks, and some want the empty photo next to the staged one. A fine amount on this page would be a guess.

What RoomGenixAI adds: RoomGenixAI does not print “Virtually staged” on the file. There is no C2PA certificate and no EXIF disclosure tag. Add whatever your board requires before you publish. That is not an approval from NAR or from an MLS.

3. Where to find your board's rule

Rules change, and they are often in member-only documents, so this page doesn't quote them. Look for the photo or listing-content section of your MLS rules, or ask your board's support desk. Large boards include:

  • CRMLS (California). California agents should also read AB 723, which applies to any digitally altered listing image.
  • Bright MLS (Mid-Atlantic)
  • Stellar MLS (Florida)
  • FMLS and GAMLS (Georgia)
  • HAR (Houston)
  • NTREIS (North Texas, including Dallas-Fort Worth)

Zillow, Redfin and other portals generally show what your MLS sends them, so the MLS rule is the one to follow. The National Association of REALTORS® Code of Ethics (Article 12) also asks members to present a true picture in their advertising.

4. A disclosure line you can adapt

If your board wants a sentence in the remarks, you can start from this and edit it to match their rule:

"Some photos in this listing have been virtually staged. Furniture and decor shown in those photos are not included with the property."

Before you publish, check every staged image against the original and follow your board's photo rules.

5. What the tool does not promise

  • It is built to keep walls, windows and floors as photographed, but it can't guarantee that on every render. Check the file. If the building moved, don't publish that file.
  • It can cover something that was in the photo. Look before you list.
  • There is no audit certificate and no board approval to download.
  • This product does not pay MLS fines.
