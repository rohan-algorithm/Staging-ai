Virtual staging for short-term rental listings

If you are selling a property to investors, or planning how to furnish a new rental, empty photos make it hard to picture the finished product. Virtual staging shows the furnished version from the photos you already have.

Where it helps

  • Selling to investors. A vacant condo near a beach or downtown is easier to pitch as a rental when the photos show it furnished the way a guest would see it.
  • Planning a furnish. Owners can try a few styles on the same photo before buying anything.
  • Pitching a property to co-hosts or partners. Show the concept without waiting for furniture.

A word of caution about guest listings

Booking platforms generally expect photos to show the place guests will actually get. If you use staged images to advertise a rental to guests before it is furnished, the guest will arrive to something different, and that can lead to complaints, refunds or problems with the platform. Use virtual staging to plan, to market a property for sale, or to show investors a concept, and clearly label it. Once the unit is furnished, photograph the real thing for guests.

Rooms that matter most

For rental-minded buyers, the rooms that sell the idea are the living area, the main bedroom and any outdoor space. A patio or balcony with seating tells a buyer this is a place people will want to stay.

Try more than one style

Because the studio keeps the room as shot and adds furniture on top, you can run the same photo in coastal, Scandinavian or farmhouse styles and compare. A coastal living room might suit a beach condo; a cleaner Scandinavian look might suit a city unit.

Workspaces sell longer stays

Tips for staging a rental-minded listing

  • Pick a style that fits the location. Coastal for beach towns, clean and simple for city units, warmer farmhouse looks for mountain or rural homes.
  • Show sleeping capacity honestly. Stage beds that would really fit the room, because investors use photos to estimate how many guests a place can take.
  • Include the outdoor space. A furnished patio or balcony shows how the property could be used, which matters to investors.
  • Keep the originals in the listing. Investors will want to see the real condition, and many MLSs require it.

From concept to real photos

Once the property is bought and furnished, reshoot it for guest listings. The staged images can still be useful as a reference for buying furniture that fits the space.

Label it wherever it appears

If staged photos are used to market a property for sale, on the MLS, a flyer or social media, label them as virtually staged and keep the unaltered originals available. Some states now require this by law; California, for example, has required a disclosure and access to the original image since January 1, 2026.

Guests expect to see the actual space, so use staged photos for planning or for selling the property, and photograph the real furnished unit for guest listings.

Can you stage outdoor spaces?

Yes. Patios and outdoor areas can be staged with outdoor furniture.
