Virtual staging for real estate photographers

You already shoot the empty rooms. Agents with vacant listings often ask whether you can "put some furniture in." RoomGenix lets you say yes in a minute or two per photo, at a cost low enough that you can price it as an add-on and keep the difference.

Why photographers add staging

Your clients already trust you with how their listing looks online. When a home is vacant, the photos you deliver are accurate but bare, and the agent then has to find a separate staging service, send the files, wait, and pay again. If you can deliver staged versions with the rest of the shoot, you save the agent a step and add a line item to the invoice.

Hand-edited virtual staging can take a day or more and is priced per image. RoomGenix runs in the browser and returns a staged photo in a minute or two, so you can stage while you edit the rest of the set and deliver everything together.

A workflow that fits a shoot

  1. Shoot the vacant rooms as you normally would. Wide, level, well-lit frames give the best results.
  2. Edit your HDR or flash-ambient set as usual.
  3. Upload the finals you want staged, pick a room type and style, and download the results.
  4. Deliver both versions. Agents usually need the unstaged originals too, for MLS rules.

More than furniture

The same credits cover other edits agents ask for: a day-to-twilight conversion of an exterior, and decluttering an occupied room so it reads as clean and open. That gives you a few extra services to offer without hiring an editor.

Keep it accurate

Agents are responsible for how a listing is advertised, and many MLSs require virtually staged photos to be labeled and the original included. The studio is designed to keep walls, windows and floors as shot, but review each image before delivery and never use staging to hide a defect. Point your clients to the MLS photo rules reminder.

How to pitch it to agents

Keep it simple. When an agent books a vacant shoot, mention that you can deliver staged versions of the key rooms with the rest of the photos, and quote a price per room or a flat add-on. Show one before-and-after from your own work rather than a stock example; agents trust results from homes in their market. If an agent hasn't used virtual staging before, remind them that they will need to label the staged photos and include the originals, and offer to deliver both sets clearly named so the MLS upload is easy.

Getting the best results from your frames

  • Shoot from a corner at roughly chest height so the floor area is visible.
  • Keep verticals straight; heavy wide-angle distortion makes furniture look off.
  • Leave the floor clear of equipment, cords and boxes.
  • Deliver the staged photo at the same size and crop as the original, so the pair lines up in the listing.
