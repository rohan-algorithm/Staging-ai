What does staging a listing actually cost?

Sellers ask, and the answer depends on which kind of staging you mean. Here is how the three common options are priced, with a worked example for a typical vacant home.

Option 1: physical staging

A stager visits, designs the rooms, and furniture is delivered and set up, then removed after the sale. You usually pay a design or setup fee and monthly furniture rental, and the rental continues as long as the home is listed. Prices vary widely by market and home size. For a vacant home, totals commonly start around a thousand dollars and can run to several thousand for larger homes or long listings. Get quotes from local stagers; national averages hide a lot of range.

Option 2: hand-edited virtual staging

An editor adds furniture to your photos by hand or with 3D software. It is priced per photo, often tens of dollars each, with a turnaround of a day or more and sometimes a charge for revisions.

A worked example

Take a vacant three-bedroom home where you want to stage six rooms: living room, dining, kitchen, primary bedroom, one spare bedroom as an office, and the patio.

  • Physical staging: a design fee plus at least a month of rental for six rooms of furniture. Expect a quote in the four figures in most markets, more if the listing runs long.
  • Hand-edited virtual: six photos at a per-photo rate, usually in the low hundreds of dollars total.
  • RoomGenix: six photos fit inside the $19 Listing pass, with two photos to spare.

What the money buys

Price isn’t the whole story. Physical staging is the only option that is there when buyers walk in, which can matter for high-end homes and in-person-heavy markets. Virtual staging only improves the photos, but the photos are where most buyers decide whether to book a showing. Many agents use virtual staging for most vacant listings and save physical staging for the few where the budget and the market justify it.

Costs people forget

  • Physical: rental extensions if the home doesn’t sell quickly, damage clauses, and coordinating access for delivery and pickup.
  • Virtual: subscriptions you don’t use, revision fees, and the time to label photos for your MLS.
