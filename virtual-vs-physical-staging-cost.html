Virtual staging vs physical staging: what it costs

Both kinds of staging help buyers picture a vacant home. They differ a lot in price, speed and what the buyer gets at the showing. Here is a plain comparison so you can choose per listing.

What physical staging costs

Physical staging means a stager designs the rooms and furniture is delivered, set up and later removed. Pricing varies widely by market, home size and how long the listing stays up, but it is usually a consultation or design fee plus monthly furniture rental. For a vacant home, total costs commonly run from around a thousand dollars into several thousand, and more for large or luxury homes or long listings. Ask local stagers for quotes; there is no single national price.

Side by side

  • Price: physical, typically hundreds to thousands; virtual with RoomGenix, about $19 for a listing.
  • Time: physical needs scheduling, delivery and setup; virtual is usually a minute or two per photo.
  • Changes: changing a physical style means moving furniture; virtual means running the photo again.
  • Showings: physical staging is there when buyers walk in; virtual staging only exists in the photos.
  • Disclosure: virtual photos usually need to be labeled under MLS rules; physical staging does not.

When physical staging is worth it

If buyers will tour mostly in person, if the home is high-end and the seller's budget allows, or if the rooms are hard to understand even when you are standing in them, real furniture can help at the showing as well as online. Some agents physically stage the main rooms and stage the rest virtually.

When virtual staging makes more sense

For most vacant listings the first job is getting buyers to book a showing, and that happens online. Virtual staging is a good fit for entry-level and mid-priced homes, rentals and condos, estate sales, homes that need to list quickly, and listings where the seller doesn't want to pay for months of furniture rental.

Hidden costs to watch

With physical staging, ask about minimum rental terms, extension fees and damage or insurance clauses. With virtual staging, watch for subscriptions you don't need and per-revision fees. RoomGenix has no subscription, credits don't expire, and you can rerun a photo if you don't like a result. Read the refund terms before you buy.

Questions to ask before deciding

  • Will most buyers see the home online first, or walk in from a yard sign or open house?
  • How long is the home likely to be on the market? Rental costs grow with time; per-photo costs don't.
  • Who is paying, the seller or the agent, and what is the marketing budget?
  • Are there rooms that are hard to understand even in person? Those may benefit from real furniture.

Whatever you choose, label virtually staged photos as your MLS and state require, and keep the originals in the listing.
